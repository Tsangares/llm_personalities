The behavior of a Language Learning Model (LLM) can be significantly influenced by the constraints imposed on it through system prompts. When 
unrestricted, the LLM tends to generate responses that are rich in theoretical context and academic language, reflecting its comprehensive training data. 
In contrast, when restricted with specific prompts, the LLM is guided to produce more practical, relatable, and contextually anchored responses. This 
study aims to analyze how different system prompts affect the LLM's approach to playing games like the Prisoner's Dilemma rather than merely thinking 
about theoretical strategies.

- **Unrestricted LLM**: The unrestricted LLM provides responses that are deeply embedded in theoretical frameworks and academic discourse. For example, 
when discussing the Prisoner's Dilemma, it might delve into game theory concepts, historical contexts, and scholarly references.
- **Restricted LLM with System Prompt**: A restricted LLM, guided by prompts such as "Think like a real person," generates responses that focus on 
immediate context and practical considerations. It refrains from theoretical elaboration and instead focuses on the decision-making process in a 
real-world scenario.

- **Unrestricted LLM**: The language used is formal and academic, replete with technical terms and complex sentence structures.
- **Restricted LLM with System Prompt**: The language becomes more conversational and relatable, using simple sentences and everyday terminology to convey 
ideas clearly and directly.

- **Unrestricted LLM**: Emphasizes theoretical analysis, exploring various strategic possibilities and the implications of different decisions within a 
game.
- **Restricted LLM with System Prompt**: Focuses on practical outcomes and immediate actions that one would take if faced with such a decision in real 
life.

By analyzing these contrasts, we can see that system prompts play a crucial role in guiding an LLM to generate responses that are more aligned with 
real-world applications rather than theoretical discussions. This approach ensures that the model's output is not only practical but also relatable, 
making it more useful for scenarios where immediate decision-making and empathy are key factors.
 -----
 
 
 
Using an unrestricted Language Learning Model (LLM) in behavioral game experiments is insufficient because it tends to generate overly theoretical and 
academically dense responses, which do not align with how humans actually make decisions in real-life scenarios. In contrast, system prompts guide the LLM 
to produce more practical, contextually relevant, and relatable outputs that better approximate human behavior. These prompts help the model focus on 
immediate consequences and intuitive decision-making processes, incorporating elements like empathy and contextual cues that are crucial for realistic 
behavioral analysis.

To quantitatively demonstrate this insufficiency, controlled experiments can be conducted where both unrestricted LLMs and system-prompted LLMs respond to 
the same set of behavioral games. Responses from human participants in similar games are then compared with those generated by the models using metrics 
such as correlation coefficients, accuracy scores, and qualitative assessments by experts. This approach highlights the discrepancies between unrestricted 
LLM outputs and genuine human behavior, underscoring the importance of system prompts for meaningful comparisons.
